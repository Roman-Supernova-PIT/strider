strider.io Module¶

Reading spectra, and writing results.

Many transients go in a table of spectra: a parquet file with one row per visit. Its columns are object_id, mjd, and wavelength, flux and error, each a list. Optional columns are mask, True for pixels to ignore, and host_redshift and host_redshift_error, the same on every row of an object. One transient can also be a CSV or ECSV file with one row per pixel (read_transient), or SMDC spectrum files, one per visit (read_smdc).

Results are written in the format the file’s ending gives: .parquet, one row per object (results_table); .csv, the same rows without the redshift posterior; .json, each object’s full result. The evidence maps go in an HDF5 file (write_evidence_maps), and the redshift posteriors in qp’s format (write_redshift_curves).

strider.preprocess Module¶

From measured prism spectra to the network’s inputs.

Each visit is prepared on its own, in float32, as in training, and as the package’s preprocessing.json describes:

  1. Native pixels with a non-finite wavelength are left out and the rest sorted by wavelength. A pixel is bad if it is masked, its flux or error is not finite, or its error is not positive.

  2. The visit scale is the lower quartile of the errors of the pixels not masked, where finite and positive.

  3. The visit is used only if its pixels cover the model’s wavelength grid, with pixel edges halfway between centres and the end edges mirrored, to within COVERAGE_SHORTFALL of the grid’s span in ln λ at each end; it has at least two good pixels; and some grid point is interpolated from good pixels. Otherwise it is dropped, and reported. At an end the visit falls short of, the grid points beyond its last pixel are masked.

  4. The flux over the scale is interpolated linearly onto the grid, and its error propagated through the same interpolation. Every grid point whose interpolation touches a bad pixel is masked; the visit is kept.

The visits are then put in time order, with times in observer-frame days since the first visit kept; visits on one date are ordered by their content, so their input order does not matter.

With the model’s noise profile, each visit kept also gets its four band fluxes and their errors (strider.bands), from its native pixels in float64, for the brightness term.

The brightness term draws its null copies from a seed of the input that does not depend on the flux unit, on the order of visits on one date, or on error_scale, so the same input, in any unit, gives the same copies:

  • σ is the median of the input errors (float64) of the good native pixels of every visit kept;

  • each visit’s tie keys are the 8-byte BLAKE2b digests, read little-endian, of rint(1000 f / (σ / error_scale)), rint(1000 e / σ) and rint(1000 λ), each as little-endian int64, and of its good-pixel mask as uint8: f and e the input flux and error of its good native pixels, λ the wavelengths of all its native pixels, in wavelength order; visits are ordered by MJD, then by the four keys in that order;

  • in that order, the seed is the 8-byte BLAKE2b digest, read little-endian, of rint(10⁶ (MJD − first MJD)) and rint(1000 ln(median of the visit’s good errors / σ)), each as little-endian int64, then the grid mask as uint8, visits by row; rint rounds halves to even.
